Answer Key

For grown-ups only: not part of the student pages
  1. Patterns: 90, 900, 9,000, then 4,200 ÷ 70 = 60 Lessons 3-1 and 3-2, pp. 96–99
    Use the basic fact, then count zeros. In part d, cross off one zero from each number (420 ÷ 7 = 60). Watch for 600, which comes from forgetting the zero in 70.
  2. Divisibility: Lessons 3-7 and 3-8, pp. 108–111
    • 687: digit sum 21; only ÷ 3 is Y (21 is not divisible by 9; odd, so no 2, 4, 6)
    • 1,260: digit sum 9; all Y (ends in 0; 60 ÷ 4 = 15; even and ÷ 3, so ÷ 6)
    • 4,716: digit sum 18; Y for 2, 3, 4, 6, 9; N for 5, 10 (16 ÷ 4 = 4; 18 is divisible by 9; ends in 6)
  3. Compatible numbers: Lesson 3-9, p. 112
    • a. 2,800 ÷ 7 = 400 frogs (28 is a basic fact with 7)
    • b. 39 is far too small. The estimate is about 400, so the answer needs three digits. (The exact answer is 391 R1. Ron's quotient is missing a digit.)
  4. Order of operations: Lesson 3-14, p. 122
    • a. 48 ÷ 8 × 3 − 4 → 6 × 3 − 4 → 18 − 4 → 14
    • b. 36 − 18 ÷ 3 × 2 + 5 → 36 − 6 × 2 + 5 → 36 − 12 + 5 → 24 + 5 → 29
      Draco subtracted 36 − 18 first. Parentheses come first, then × and ÷ from left to right, and only then + and −.
      Also watch for 38 (did 3 × 2 before 18 ÷ 3, reading PEMDAS as “multiply before divide”) and 19 (did 12 + 5 before 36 − 12, reading it as “add before subtract”).
  5. Zeros in the quotient: 4,839 ÷ 6 = 806 R3    3,248 ÷ 8 = 406 Lessons 3-5 and 3-6, pp. 104–107
    Check: 806 × 6 + 3 = 4,839   406 × 8 = 3,248
    Watch for 86 R3 and 46: the tens step has a 0 that must be written in the quotient.
  6. $183.15 ÷ 37 = $4.95 per present Lesson 3-13, p. 120
    Place the $ and the decimal point in the quotient first, then divide as usual. Check: 37 × $4.95 = $183.15.
  7. 103 ÷ 4 = 25 R3 → 26 boats (round up) Lesson 3-3, p. 100
    25 boats leave 3 students on the shore, so they need one more boat. If your child answers 27 because Hagrid rides in a boat of his own (as in the book), that is good reading: accept it if the 26 is in their work.
  8. 315 ÷ 12 = 26 R3 → 26 batches (round down) Lesson 3-10, p. 114
    The 3 leftover fangs are not enough for another batch of 12.
  9. 1,150 ÷ 24 = 47 R22 Lesson 3-11, p. 116
    a. 47 boxes (round down)    b. 22 beans left over
    The 22 leftover beans can't fill a box of 24, so they don't add a box. In part b the remainder itself is the answer.
  10. 20,000 ÷ 36 = 555 R20 → 556 boxes (round up) Lesson 3-12, p. 118
    555 boxes is only 19,980 candles, 20 short. He can't buy part of a box, so he needs one more whole box.
